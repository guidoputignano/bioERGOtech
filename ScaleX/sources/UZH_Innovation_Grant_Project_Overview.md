## Project title

Functional Drug Screening in Patient-Derived Organoids for Precision Treatment Selection in Gastric Cancer

## Problem

Gastric cancer patients can respond very differently to the same systemic treatment. Molecular and genomic profiling can identify relevant alterations, but these data do not always provide a clear prediction of which available drug will be effective for an individual patient. At the same time, the development and prioritization of new therapeutic strategies remain challenging, highlighting the need for approaches that integrate molecular insights with functional drug testing. Complementary functional approaches that directly assess drug responses in patient-derived tumour models could help bridge this gap, with potential applications in both precision treatment selection and drug development.

## Solution

The project aims to develop and validate a patient-derived organoid (PDO)-based functional drug-screening approach for gastric cancer. Tumour biopsies are used to establish PDOs, which can be characterized molecularly and exposed to a panel of clinically relevant oncology drugs through automated high-throughput screening. By integrating molecular and genomic profiling with direct functional assessment of drug responses, the platform aims to generate patient-specific response profiles that complement existing precision-oncology approaches. The analysis will relate functional responses to molecular features, tumour characteristics and treatment history, helping define which response patterns may provide clinically relevant information. In parallel, functional screening may identify and prioritize therapeutic strategies associated with specific molecular features, creating a link between patient-specific treatment selection and drug development. Promising genotype–drug response relationships can be further evaluated using organoid-derived tumour models in vivo.

## Technology Status

The project builds on three years of scientific and technical expertise developed within the UZH research environment. Protocols for the generation, culture and genetic engineering of human gastric patient-derived organoids (PDOs) have been established in collaboration with the Departments of Visceral Surgery and Pathology at USZ, enabling the generation of organoids from patient tumour material. In parallel, workflows for large-scale functional drug screening in 3D organoid models have been established through collaboration with experts at the Children’s Hospital Zurich, including screening of clinically relevant oncology drug libraries.

These approaches have already identified differential drug responses associated with specific genetic backgrounds, with the vast majority of hits individually validated. Organoid-derived tumour and xenograft models further provide a framework for in vivo validation of promising genotype–drug response relationships.

The experimental foundation is therefore established. The Innovation Grant would support the next translational step: validating clinical and market needs, refining the value proposition and defining a realistic pathway towards implementation in precision oncology.

## Innovation Potential

The key innovation is the integration of functional drug-response testing with molecular information in patient-derived gastric cancer models. Current precision-oncology approaches often rely primarily on molecular biomarkers to infer treatment response, whereas this approach directly measures how a patient’s tumour-derived model responds to clinically relevant therapies, generating functional information that can complement genomic data.

The analysis translates these measurements into interpretable response profiles by comparing standardized drug responses across patients and relating them to tumour characteristics, treatment history and genetic background. This could identify response patterns relevant to treatment selection, while also revealing associations between molecular features and drug response that may support patient stratification and therapeutic prioritization.

The platform therefore has complementary applications for hospitals and oncologists, as well as pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies. Where matched normal organoids are available, functional testing may additionally provide information on tumour selectivity.

In the longer term, standardized molecular and functional datasets could enable data-driven and AI-based approaches for response profiling, biomarker discovery and therapeutic prioritization, supporting the evolution from an experimental screening service towards a scalable precision-oncology platform.

## Based on UZH research

This project is directly based on research conducted during my PhD at the University of Zurich, where I have developed extensive expertise in gastric cancer organoid models and their application to tumour biology and therapeutic research. My work has included the generation and genetic engineering of murine gastric organoids, their use in in vivo CRISPR screening and tumour models, and the identification and validation of tumour suppressors and therapeutic vulnerabilities. Building on this expertise, I have established protocols for generating and engineering human gastric patient-derived organoids from clinical tumour material in collaboration with USZ. In parallel, I have developed functional drug-screening workflows in 3D organoid models through collaboration with the Children’s Hospital Zurich, identifying differential drug responses associated with specific genetic backgrounds and validating the robustness of these findings.

Together, these UZH research activities provide the experimental foundation for the proposed innovation. The Innovation Grant would enable the next step: translating this established research platform into an integrated precision-oncology approach and systematically assessing its clinical, translational and market potential.

## Expected Outcome

At the end of the grant period, the project should have a better-defined value proposition, validated market needs and a clearer implementation strategy for translating the research-based platform towards clinical and commercial applications. The validation activities will define the needs of potential users and partners, including hospitals, oncologists, pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies, university spin-offs and academic research laboratories.

A key outcome will be a clearer understanding of what functional response information clinicians would consider relevant for treatment decisions, how such information should be communicated, and what value standardized molecular and drug-response datasets could provide to industry partners. These insights will directly inform the positioning and future design of the platform.

The results will provide an evidence-based foundation for subsequent funding, strategic partnerships and further development. In the longer term, accumulated molecular and functional response data could support data-driven and AI-based approaches for response prediction and therapeutic prioritization, subject to sufficient data and successful validation.

## Use of funding

The grant would be used to validate the innovation beyond the academic proof-of-concept by systematically assessing clinical needs, user requirements, potential industry applications and the value proposition of the proposed platform. Activities may include market and competitor analysis, interviews and discussions with potential users and partners, including hospitals, oncologists, pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies, university spin-offs and academic research laboratories.

Funding would also support refinement of the future product and service concept, including the positioning of functional drug-response profiling within existing precision-oncology workflows and assessment of relevant clinical, regulatory, data-management and commercial requirements.

An important part of the validation will be understanding what evidence clinicians require before considering functional response information in treatment decisions, what value industry partners derive from standardized molecular and drug-response datasets, and which requirements would need to be addressed for future clinical implementation.

The aim is not primarily to generate additional academic data, but to determine how the established research platform can create measurable value for relevant stakeholders and define a realistic pathway towards implementation.

## Team

The project will be conducted by a multidisciplinary team combining scientific, computational and entrepreneurial expertise. Giovanni Papa will lead the project, bringing three years of experience in gastric cancer organoid models, functional drug screening, molecular characterization and precision oncology developed during his PhD at UZH, together with experience in entrepreneurship and translational research.

Guido Putignano, MSc ETH in Computational Biology and Bioinformatics and President of bioERGOtech will contribute expertise in computational biology, bioinformatics and data analysis. Lorenzo Scalise, with a background in finance and business development and an MBA from the University of St. Gallen, will contribute expertise in business development, go-to-market strategy, stakeholder engagement and partnership development.

Prof. Anne Müller, Giovanni’s PhD supervisor and scientific mentor, will provide scientific guidance and support the integration of the project within the UZH research environment. The team will also leverage established collaborations with clinical and drug-screening partners to access relevant expertise and stakeholder perspectives.

No additional hire is currently planned, as the core team provides the complementary expertise required for the proposed validation activities.

## Outlook

Following the UZH Innovation Grant, the next step would be to further develop and validate the platform through strategic clinical and industry partnerships. Based on the market validation results, the project would progress towards a clearly defined product or service model and seek follow-up funding and entrepreneurial support, potentially through the UZH Bioentrepreneur Fellowship, to advance its technical, translational and commercial development.

In the medium term, the goal is to establish a scalable precision-oncology pipeline integrating patient-derived organoids, molecular profiling and functional drug screening, initially focusing on gastric cancer and potentially expanding to other gastrointestinal malignancies. Partnerships with hospitals, pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies could support applications in patient stratification, biomarker discovery and drug development.

As standardized datasets accumulate, data-driven approaches could progressively be developed to identify genotype–drug response relationships and, subject to sufficient sample sizes and successful validation, explore predictive response models. Ultimately, the vision is to translate the research platform into a clinically and commercially relevant precision-oncology solution and establish OncoTarget Precision Oncology as a scalable venture.
